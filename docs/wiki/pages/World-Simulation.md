# World simulation

Enable **World simulation** in a world's **Overview → World modules** to give stories a clock, calendar, day planner, weather, schedules, and scene flags. It starts enabled for Visual Novel and Dating Sim templates and off for Freeform RP; you can change it.

## Set up a believable day

Use **Worlds → Simulation → World clock** to see or advance the shared world clock. Use **Cast → World Life** for a character's schedule and weather preferences. The Simulation tab also holds custom scene flags and rules that run when their conditions hold. Keep the simulation small at first: a few locations and cast routines are easier to verify than a full calendar.

## Calendar

Every world starts on the built-in year: four seasons of 28 days, Monday to Sunday, and a holiday in the middle of each season. **Worlds → Simulation → Calendar → Use this world's own calendar** replaces it for that world:

- **Year:** the year the clock starts in, and an optional era. "X" shows as *X792*, "AC" as *AC 792*; choose **After the year** for *792 AC*.
- **Months:** names and lengths (up to 24 months and 1,000 days a year). Each month's season sets the weather; left on *From its place*, it follows where the month falls in the year.
- **Days of the week:** rename the seven days. Weeks stay seven days long so character schedules keep working, and the last two days are the weekend.
- **Holidays:** a name, a month and a day.

Characters and the Game Master are told the date. **World clock → Set date** moves the clock to any day, keeping the time of day; setting a date also saves the calendar. Birthdays (**Cast → Life & background**) are a month and day of the world's year. **Go back to the built-in calendar** removes the world's own.

## Advancing time while you play

Tick **World clock → Advance time while role playing**, then save the world, and the clock follows the story:

- **Narrated time.** When your message says time passes, such as "that evening", "the next morning", "two days later", "hours pass" or "by dusk", the clock moves there. Naming an earlier part of the day means the next day; the clock never goes back. Words inside "quotes" don't count, so a character saying "see you tomorrow" doesn't move it, and neither does merely mentioning a time ("good night", "this morning").
- **Game Master rulings.** In Game Master scenes, your message and the Game Master's narration are read together, so a time skip moves the clock once. The Game Master is told to say plainly when time passes.
- **Ending a scene** moves the clock on one part of the day.

A short notice says where the clock moved. Characters' own replies don't move it.

All stories in a simulated world use the same day, time of day, season, and weather. During play, **Story panel → Scene** shows time and upcoming key dates; **Tools → Director** can advance to the next phase. The day planner appears with dating controls. If time or place is wrong, correct the scene context and review the schedule or trigger that caused it.

Simulation is optional. Disabling it hides its controls while retaining the world's saved settings. A freeform story can still describe time in prose without a tracked clock. For consequences that must be mechanically recorded, use [Story State and Continuity](https://github.com/guswadsworth12/Lost-Tale-Engine/wiki/Story-State-and-Continuity).
